# Write analysis that earns its conclusion

Answer the actual question with reasoning the reader can inspect. Preserve the user's source requirements, disciplinary conventions, citation format, and requested degree of detail. A useful conclusion may be conditional or unresolved.

## Frame the question and record

Identify the decision, ambiguity, disagreement, or pattern the writing must explain. Determine which sources and comparison criteria the user supplied. Clarify only an omission that prevents a meaningful answer; otherwise state a limited assumption and continue with the supported work.

When the assignment is closed-book, use its materials rather than importing outside facts. When research is authorized and needed, inspect sources with available tools and record what each supports. Without source access, state that limitation and distinguish what can still be inferred. Never fabricate citations, page numbers, quotations, or the appearance of a search.

## Develop the reasoning

Separate observations, source claims, interpretations, assumptions, and recommendations in the language of the prose. Do not require a separate heading for each category unless it helps the reader. Explain what a source contributes: context, evidence, a competing explanation, or a limit on the conclusion.

Compare alternatives on the same relevant basis. Keep units, dates, populations, definitions, and conditions visible when they affect the comparison. Check arithmetic. A lower listed price does not establish better support or lower lifetime cost. Disagreement may arise from different questions or values rather than incompatible facts.

Connect evidence to the claim it supports. Explain the inference rather than placing a quotation beside an assertion and leaving the relationship unstated. Consider the strongest plausible alternative interpretation and material contrary evidence. Revise the conclusion when they require it; avoid manufacturing balance where the record is one-sided.

State uncertainty precisely. Name the missing fact, assumption, or scope limit that matters and how it could change the answer. Avoid both blanket hedging and confident generalization beyond the evidence. Preserve the difference between an association and a demonstrated cause.

Choose the structure the question requires: a direct finding with support, criterion-based comparison, thematic synthesis, or argument. Use a table for a genuine mapping or comparison. Avoid a source-by-source catalogue when the reader needs synthesis.

## Examples

Unsupported: “Attendance rose, proving the longer hours worked.”

Supported: “Attendance rose after hours changed. The available survey does not establish whether the new hours caused the increase.”

Unbounded: “Plan A is best.”

Qualified: “For 15 users, Plan A costs $50 a month and Plan B costs $120. Choose A on current price and the stated feature requirements; export and support terms remain unverified.”

## Check the argument

Make one targeted revision for the weakest inference, missing comparison, or buried finding. Verify that each important claim has support or is clearly marked as an assumption or inference. Keep the conclusion and its meaningful limits together. Return the requested analysis, with citations where required and gaps proportionate to their importance.
